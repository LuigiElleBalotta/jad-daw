# How Logic Pro shows Flex (our own notes)

From Apple's guide (text and its figures, seen for reference, none of it kept here) and from the shape of the editor.

- **Where:** the Audio Track Editor (double click an audio region, or the Editors button). Its menu bar has the Edit / Functions / View menus, the **Flex** button (Show/Hide Flex), the **Flex Mode** pop-up to the right of it, the tool buttons and the zoom. With Flex Pitch on, an **inspector on the left** holds the quantize of time and pitch, the pitch correction and the gain.
- **Flex Time:** the transient markers are faded grey lines in the waveform; a flex marker is a white line with an orange handle. The Flex tool grabs the waveform; the movement is held between the transient markers on both sides.
- **Flex Pitch:** a piano keyboard at the left edge and a grid with a row for every semitone (the black-key rows a little darker); the waveform is a dim blue picture behind; every note is a blue rounded block with a thin darker strip along its bottom (its gain), a lighter blue when selected; the **pitch curve** of the note (vibrato, drift) is drawn over it as a light line; six hotspots (small round handles) appear on a note: drift in, vibrato, drift out, gain, fine pitch, formant shift. Drag vertically for the pitch (it lands on a semitone), horizontally to move, an edge to resize; Scissors cut, Glue joins.
- **What we have of it:** the Flex button and mode pop-up in the track header and in the editor, the Flex view (Edit > Show Flex Pitch/Time), the Flex tool, transient markers (faded) and flex markers (white, orange handle), the Flex Editor window and the Track tab of the Editors area with the keyboard, the rows, the dim waveform, blue notes with the gain strip, the curve and the inspector on the left. The six hotspots are sliders in the inspector, not handles on the note.
